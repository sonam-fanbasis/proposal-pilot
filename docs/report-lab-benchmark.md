## ✅ What does Chapter 3 say about sovereign debt in developing economies?
*2 · Long, structured report — Global Economic Prospects -- June 2026*  
route: `explicit` CHAPTER 3, Ch. 3 A Rising Challenge: Sovereign Debt Levels and Interest Rates in EMDEs · first relevant rank: 1 · scope precision: 1.0 · evidence types: note, text · confidence 0.994

Rising government debt in emerging market and developing economies (EMDEs) poses a key challenge by leading to higher interest rates, increased debt-service payments, and a greater likelihood of debt distress, with aggregate government debt rising from less than 40 percent of GDP in 2010 to over 70 percent [1][3]. 

* **Debt-Service Costs:** The cost of servicing government debt increased from 6 percent of government revenues in 2010 to an estimated 11 percent in 2025, which can divert resources from priority uses, require additional borrowing, increase the economy-wide cost of capital, and potentially trigger debt distress or default [3].
* **Interest Rate Impacts:** Debt levels are positively associated with dollar-denominated sovereign bond spreads and domestic-currency government bond yields in a non-linear relationship where increases in debt-to-GDP ratios lead to greater impacts at higher debt levels [1]. The rise in EMDE debt-to-GDP ratios since 2010 is associated with increases of about 110 basis points in sovereign spreads and 30 basis points in domestic-currency yields [1].
* **Vulnerabilities and Spillovers:** Rising debt has led to even larger interest rate increases in countries with a history of default, low credit ratings, frontier market status, heavy reliance on short-term debt, and weak governance, while rising advanced economy debt has added further upward pressure on EMDE interest rates [1].
* **Policy Implications:** Recommendations to strengthen fiscal positions include domestic revenue mobilization, efficient public spending, improved debt management, domestic debt market development, and debt-for-development swaps [1].

> **Reference:** Rising government debt raises EMDE interest rates (spreads and domestic yields), debt-service costs and distress risk; the effect is non-linear; the post-2010 debt rise is associated with ~110 bp higher spreads and ~30 bp higher yields.
